Keeping a human decision in AI hiring: documenting overrides so they hold up

Published 2026-10-30T00:00:00-04:00 ·

As AI hiring systems become more prevalent, it's essential to ensure that human decision-making is still a crucial part of the process. This is particularly important when it comes to overrides, where a human recruiter or hiring manager chooses to disregard the AI's recommendation. In order to maintain fairness and transparency, it's vital to document these overrides in a way that can withstand scrutiny.

Why the override is the real decision

When an AI system makes a recommendation, it's based on the data and algorithms used to train it. However, the final decision is always made by a human. If the human chooses to override the AI's recommendation, it's essential to recognize that this override is, in fact, the real decision. By documenting the override, recruiters and hiring managers can provide a clear explanation for why they chose to go against the AI's suggestion, which helps to ensure accountability and fairness in the hiring process.

What to record at the moment of override

When documenting an override, it's crucial to capture the following information: the AI's recommendation, the human decision, the reason for the override, and any evidence that supports this decision. By recording this information, recruiters and hiring managers can provide a clear audit trail that explains why the override was made. This not only helps to ensure that the hiring process is fair and unbiased but also provides a valuable learning opportunity for the AI system, allowing it to improve its recommendations over time.

Anchored reason codes instead of free text

Using anchored reason codes instead of free text when documenting overrides is highly recommended. Anchored reason codes provide a standardized framework for explaining why an override was made, which helps to reduce ambiguity and ensure consistency. By using a predefined set of reason codes, recruiters and hiring managers can ensure that their explanations are clear, concise, and easily understandable. This also makes it easier to review and analyze override patterns, which can help to identify potential biases or areas for improvement in the hiring process.

Who may override and who may not

It's essential to establish clear guidelines on who is authorized to override the AI's recommendations and under what circumstances. Typically, this should be limited to senior recruiters or hiring managers who have the necessary expertise and experience to make informed decisions. By restricting override authority to specific individuals, organizations can help ensure that overrides are made for legitimate reasons and that the hiring process remains fair and unbiased. It's also crucial to ensure that individuals who are not authorized to override the AI's recommendations do not attempt to do so, as this can undermine the integrity of the hiring process.

Reviewing override patterns for bias

Regularly reviewing override patterns is essential to ensure that the hiring process remains fair and unbiased. By analyzing the reasons for overrides, organizations can identify potential biases or disparities in the hiring process. For example, if overrides are consistently being made for certain demographic groups or job types, this may indicate a problem with the AI system or the hiring process as a whole. By identifying and addressing these issues, organizations can take steps to mitigate bias and ensure that their hiring process is fair and equitable for all candidates.

Retention of the record

Finally, it's crucial to ensure that override records are retained for a sufficient period, in accordance with relevant laws and regulations. This provides a clear audit trail that can be used to demonstrate fairness and transparency in the hiring process. By maintaining accurate and detailed records of overrides, organizations can provide evidence that their hiring decisions are fair, unbiased, and based on legitimate business needs. This not only helps to protect the organization from potential legal challenges but also promotes a culture of fairness and accountability in the hiring process.
