Writing a student AI acceptable-use agreement: a clause-by-clause walkthrough

Published 2026-10-01 ·

To craft a student AI acceptable-use agreement that balances clarity with practicality, focus on three core areas: defining scope and boundaries for AI tools, addressing academic integrity and consequences, and ensuring data privacy and teacher discretion. Below is a clause-by-clause breakdown tailored for administrators to draft an agreement students and parents will understand. Use this as a checklist to review and refine each section before finalizing.

Start with a clear scope of allowed tools. Avoid vague terms like "AI" and instead specify what students may use, such as chatbots for research, code generation for programming projects, or creative writing assistance. Include examples of prohibited uses, like AI-generated essays submitted as original work or AI tools used to manipulate images or videos for academic purposes. A practical approach is to list permitted tools by category (e.g., "Khan Academy’s AI tutor," "Grammarly for editing," "MidJourney for art projects") and explicitly state that students must not use AI to fabricate content or deceive teachers.

Next, establish academic-integrity boundaries. Clarify that AI can be used for learning purposes, such as brainstorming ideas, checking facts, or improving writing, but students must acknowledge its use and cite sources appropriately. Define what constitutes a violation—such as submitting AI-generated work without disclosure, using AI to alter assignments beyond minor edits, or exploiting AI to gain an unfair advantage. Provide examples of acceptable vs. unacceptable practices to help students grasp the distinction.

Address data and privacy clauses with specificity. Explain that student-generated data, including chat logs, images, or voice recordings, will be stored and used solely for educational purposes. If your district uses AI tools that collect data, outline how that data is handled, including who has access and for how long. Emphasize that students have the right to request deletion of their data and that teachers will not share student-specific information outside the classroom unless required by law. Use language like, "Your data will be protected in compliance with [FERPA/COPPA/state law], and we will not sell or share it with third parties."

Delegate teacher discretion by stating that while the agreement sets guidelines, final decisions on AI use will rest with educators. Provide teachers with clear criteria to assess AI use, such as whether the tool enhances learning or undermines academic integrity. Include a clause that allows teachers to modify rules for specific subjects or projects, ensuring flexibility without undermining consistency. Example: "Teachers may approve limited AI use in certain courses, provided it aligns with the district’s AI policy and does not compromise academic standards."

Design the violation handling process to focus on education rather than punishment. Outline a tiered response system: first, a warning for minor infractions; second, a meeting with the student and parent to discuss consequences and alternatives; third, suspension or expulsion for repeated violations or severe offenses. Emphasize that the goal is to teach responsibility, not just enforce rules. Include a clause on restorative practices, such as requiring students to complete an assignment using only allowed tools or participate in a reflection activity to understand the impact of AI misuse.

Ensure the agreement is signable and reviewable. Provide a section where students and parents sign off, with parents acknowledging they have read and understood the terms. Include a clause that the agreement will be reviewed annually, with updates based on new AI tools, legal changes, or district policies. Use language like, "This agreement will be reviewed annually, and new terms will be communicated to students and parents via the district’s website and email."

To streamline the process, use the following decision rules for drafting:

  • Define permitted tools explicitly—avoid broad statements like "AI tools allowed" and instead list examples and restrictions.
  • Clarify academic integrity—distinguish between learning tools and cheating, with clear examples of each.
  • Protect data privacy—state how data is stored, shared, and protected, including compliance with relevant laws.
  • Empower teachers—give educators authority to approve or deny AI use on a case-by-case basis.
  • Focus on education—structure consequences to teach accountability rather than punish.
  • Ensure sign-off and review—include a parent-student acknowledgment and an annual review process.

Before finalizing, test the agreement with students and parents to ensure clarity. Conduct a pilot review in one classroom or district-wide group to gather feedback on readability and relevance. Adjust phrasing as needed to avoid legal risks or confusion. For example, if a clause is unclear, rephrase it to avoid ambiguity, such as: "Students may use AI tools to draft or edit assignments, but they must submit the final work as their own original work."
