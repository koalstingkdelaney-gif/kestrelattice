Incident metrics that matter: measuring detection, containment, and recovery for AI agents

Published 2026-10-30T00:00:00-04:00 ·

Incident response for AI agents like IncidentLattice is crucial to minimize the impact of potential incidents on business operations and reputation. Measuring incident response effectiveness is key to identifying areas for improvement and optimizing the incident response process. Incident metrics provide valuable insights into the performance of the incident response team and the overall resilience of the organization. In this context, it's essential to measure the clock in three segments: detection, containment, and recovery.

Measure the Clock in Three Segments

Measuring the time it takes for each segment of the incident response process helps identify bottlenecks and areas for improvement. The three segments are: detection (the time it takes to identify an incident), containment (the time it takes to prevent the incident from spreading), and recovery (the time it takes to restore normal business operations). By measuring these segments separately, organizations can focus on optimizing each stage of the incident response process.

Detection Metrics

Detection metrics are critical in evaluating the effectiveness of the incident response team's ability to identify potential incidents. Key detection metrics include the time it takes to detect an incident (mean time to detect, MTTD) and the percentage of incidents detected by automated systems versus manual reporting. These metrics help organizations assess their monitoring capabilities, anomaly detection, and incident identification processes.

Containment Metrics

Containment metrics assess the incident response team's ability to prevent the spread of an incident and minimize its impact. The mean time to contain (MTTC) is a crucial metric, measuring the time it takes to isolate the incident and prevent further damage. Other important metrics include the number of systems or data affected during the containment phase and the effectiveness of containment strategies. These metrics provide insights into the team's ability to respond quickly and effectively to incidents.

Recovery Metrics

Recovery metrics evaluate the incident response team's ability to restore normal business operations after an incident. The mean time to recover (MTTR) is a key metric, measuring the time it takes to restore systems, data, and business processes. Other important recovery metrics include the number of users or customers affected during the recovery phase, the effectiveness of backup and restore processes, and the overall downtime experienced by the organization. These metrics help assess the team's ability to minimize business disruption and ensure continuity.

Quality Metrics Beyond Time

While time-based metrics (MTTD, MTTC, MTTR) are essential, quality metrics provide additional insights into the incident response process. The blast radius, which measures the scope and impact of an incident, is a critical metric. Repeat incidents, which indicate the effectiveness of root cause analysis and remediation, are also important. Other quality metrics include the number of incidents caused by human error, the effectiveness of communication with stakeholders, and the overall customer experience during and after an incident. These metrics help organizations assess the quality of their incident response process and identify areas for improvement.

Setting Review Thresholds that Trigger Action

Setting review thresholds for incident metrics is crucial to trigger action and drive improvements in the incident response process. Organizations should establish thresholds for each metric, such as MTTD, MTTC, and MTTR, and review them regularly. When thresholds are exceeded, the incident response team should conduct a thorough review of the incident response process to identify areas for improvement. This helps ensure that the organization is continually learning from incidents and optimizing its incident response capabilities.

A Small Starter Dashboard List

A starter dashboard for incident metrics should include a range of key metrics to provide insights into the incident response process. Some essential metrics to include are:

  • Mean time to detect (MTTD)
  • Mean time to contain (MTTC)
  • Mean time to recover (MTTR)
  • Blast radius (scope and impact of incidents)
  • Repeat incidents (effectiveness of root cause analysis and remediation)
  • Incident frequency and trend analysis
  • Incident response team performance and feedback
These metrics provide a solid foundation for evaluating the effectiveness of the incident response process and identifying areas for improvement.
