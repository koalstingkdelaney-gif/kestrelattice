How to read the heatmap

Each cell is one exposure driver. Its colour combines your configuration (domain, mesh, autonomy, data) with whether the matching control exists. A missing control hurts most where the configuration is hottest — a fully autonomous mesh making Annex III decisions with no tested stop procedure is the reddest cell this generator can produce.

  • Human oversight (Art. 14): high-risk systems need named humans who can monitor, interpret, override, and stop them — across every agent, not just the first one.
  • Record-keeping (Art. 12): logs must reconstruct what the mesh did, including which agent delegated what to whom.
  • Risk management (Art. 9) & data governance (Art. 10): shared memory and delegation do not dilute these duties — they extend them to every agent that touches the data.
  • Transparency (Art. 13 & 50): deployers and affected people need to know an agent mesh is acting, what it can do, and where its limits are.

FAQ

Is this legal advice?

No. This tool is an informational self-assessment for operational readiness — it is not legal advice and does not classify your system.

Does my input leave my device?

No. The generator runs entirely in your browser and stores nothing.

Why does mesh size matter?

Because duties attach to the system, while failures happen at hand-offs. Every delegation is a point where oversight, logging, and data controls can drop — larger, more open meshes have more of those points.

What should I fix first?

Red and critical cells first, in heatmap order. Then document the fix, keep the evidence, and re-run this generator whenever the mesh, its autonomy, or its data changes.
