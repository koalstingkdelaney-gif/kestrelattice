What Article 14 actually requires

Article 14 requires high-risk AI systems to be designed so they can be effectively overseen by natural persons while in use. Oversight must be proportionate to the risks, the level of autonomy, and the context of use. The people assigned to oversight must be able to understand the system's capacities and limitations, monitor its operation and detect anomalies, stay aware of automation bias, correctly interpret its output, and — critically — decide not to use it, disregard or override its output, and intervene or interrupt it, including through a stop procedure.

  • Understandability (Art. 14(4)(a)): overseers understand capacities, limitations, and risks.
  • Monitoring & anomaly detection (Art. 14(4)(b)): operation is watched; dysfunctions and unexpected performance are detected.
  • Automation-bias awareness (Art. 14(4)(c)): overseers are trained not to over-rely on the output.
  • Interpretation (Art. 14(4)(d)): output can be correctly interpreted, using the tools and information provided.
  • Override & interruption (Art. 14(4)(e)): overseers can disregard, override, or reverse output and stop the system safely.

An audit trail is what proves any of this happened. Without timestamped records of who oversaw what, which output was overridden, and when the system was stopped, oversight exists on paper only.
