How the numbers work

Total keys = agents × keys per agent. Shadow spend is the slice of your monthly spend attributable to dormant keys: total monthly spend × dormant-key share. That is spend capacity provisioned to keys nobody used — the first place to cut, and the first place an auditor will look. Permission risk rises with the share of keys holding write, delete, or payment power and falls as per-key logging improves: an over-privileged key you cannot trace is exposure without evidence.

The audit order that works: inventory every key per agent, revoke dormant keys, downgrade write keys to the narrowest scope the agent's logged activity proves it needs, split shared keys so every call maps to one agent, and put a monthly per-key spend ceiling in place so a single key cannot silently become the budget.

This is an informational self-assessment based only on the numbers you enter — not a security audit and not financial advice. Verify key usage in your providers' own consoles before revoking anything a production agent may still need.

FAQ

What is shadow spend for AI agents?

Here it means spend capacity tied to dormant or forgotten API keys — provisioned, permissioned, and billable, but unused in the last 30 days. It is calculated only from your own spend and dormant-share inputs.

Does my input leave my device?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser, and the PDF is generated by your browser's print function.

Why does permission breadth matter if spend is low?

Spend measures cost; permissions measure blast radius. A low-spend key with delete or payment power can still cause the expensive incident — the audit scores both, separately.
